1. Dataset overview

Dataset name: customer-churn-training.csv

Purpose: Explore customer churn patterns in a small training sample. The target column is churned (1 = churned, 0 = active).

Record count: 12 customers. Feature count: 6 input fields plus the customer identifier and target field.

Fields: customer_id, tenure_months, support_tickets, monthly_spend_inr, last_login_days, plan_type, churned.

2. Descriptive findings

In this sample, customers marked as churned had a shorter average tenure, more support tickets on average, and more days since their last login than active customers. These are associations in this dataset, not proof that any one factor caused churn.

4. Intended and inappropriate uses

Intended uses

  • Educational data analysis and visualization.
  • Practicing data cleaning, descriptive statistics, and responsible documentation.
  • Generating hypotheses for further investigation with a larger, representative dataset.

Not appropriate for

  • Making automated decisions about an individual customer.
  • Penalizing customers, denying service, or changing prices based only on this sample.
  • Claiming reliable predictive performance or causal relationships.

5. Data quality and limitations

  • The supplied file contains 12 rows and 7 columns.
  • No missing values were found in the supplied sample.
  • Customer IDs are identifiers and should not be treated as predictive signals.
  • The sample is small; observed patterns may be unstable and may not represent the wider customer base.
  • The file does not provide collection dates, sampling methodology, geographic coverage, or consent/provenance details.

6. Privacy, fairness and responsible use

  • Keep customer-level records private and share only aggregated results where possible.
  • Do not attempt to identify people from customer IDs or combine this file with external personal data.
  • Check data provenance, permissions, retention rules, and access controls before any real-world use.
  • Assess model performance across relevant groups if suitable, lawful, and consented group data becomes available.
  • Provide human review and a way to challenge consequential decisions.

7. Recommended next steps

  1. Obtain a larger, representative dataset with documented collection and consent details.
  2. Validate data definitions, time windows, and the meaning of the churn label.
  3. Use a train/validation/test split only when sample size is sufficient.
  4. Evaluate precision, recall, calibration, and subgroup performance before considering a predictive model.
  5. Review privacy, fairness, and business impact with responsible stakeholders.
